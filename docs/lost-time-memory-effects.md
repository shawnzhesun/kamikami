# Lost Time Memory: visual treatment study

Source: the local file “じん-ロスタイムメモリー-IA-OFFICIAL-MUSIC-VIDEO”, 1920 × 1080, 24 fps, 4:43.40. Analyzed October 7, 2026. All subjects are treated as drawings, text, or animated cutouts.

## Method and scope

Reviewed timestamped samples across the entire movie at one frame per second, with denser four-frame-per-second sequences around 00:05, 00:36, 01:14, 01:34, 02:28, 03:51, 04:00, and 04:16. This identifies recurring treatment families and several fast transitions; it is not an exhaustive inspection of every source frame. The approximate times below are reference examples, not measured transition boundaries.

A flattened video cannot reveal its original layers, masks, keyframes, fonts, or effect settings. These 33 presets are adjustable reconstructions of the observed visual vocabulary. They join the three existing presets in the built-in library. None of the video's drawings or audio are bundled with the application.

## Current library organization

The catalog below records the original 33 video-derived recipes. Related recipes are now variations of five shared cards: Color flash, Color silhouette, Color grade, Scene light, and Camera zoom. Together with the retained presets, that consolidation produced 30 built-in cards. The subsequent [second video study](videoplayback-effects.md) adds four unique operations for a total of 34. Search by an original name to find its family, then select that look in the Variation dropdown. See [the full consolidation audit](effect-library-audit.md). Existing project recipes remain unchanged.

## Rebuilding representative shots

- **Opening title (00:00–00:10):** use a dark background drawing with Paper spotlight. Place the title above it and apply Title fragments out during its final 1.2 seconds. Soft iris in on the following background approximates the dark-to-light reveal. The original individually designed shards require custom artwork; the preset uses 24 triangular pieces.
- **Distorted title / monitor sequence (00:36–00:47):** apply Broken TV signal to the screen artwork, then transition into CRT phosphor. Animate the screen with Scene pullback and Angled picture plane; place a separately drawn monitor frame above it. Hexagon field belongs on its own background drawing or plate for the graphic cutaway.
- **Cold character shots (00:49–01:13):** Midnight teal on the background, Drawing pan at different strengths on separate foreground/background illustrations, and a restrained Horizon flare. Keep lyric text separate when it should remain crisp and neutral.
- **Fast red/cyan montage (01:14–01:21):** short clips with Red silhouette on isolated drawings; Graphic panel in or Whip slide in on incoming shots; Impact zoom and Chromatic fringe for accented cuts. The surrounding sequence still needs explicit edits in the timeline.
- **Warm memories (02:09–02:19):** Sunset memory on the scene, restrained Soft highlight bloom, Floating paper flecks on the background, and a slow Drawing pan. Use a separate character layer to keep the light and particles behind the subject.
- **Rotating paired composition (03:15–03:27):** arrange both drawings around a shared center and apply matching Orbiting composition settings to both, with Paper spotlight behind them. The app does not introduce a new group/precomposition feature; a previously composited image is another option.
- **Final montage and ending (03:51–04:43):** mix short White flash in / Red flash in shots, Cutout echoes on transparent figures, Kinetic title entry on words, and Scene pullback / Angled picture plane on the final drawing. Fade to dark over a black background for the last shot.

## Applying and adjusting

Open **Effects**, search by name or description, or choose a category. Hover a sample or press Play to preview; drag a card onto a visual timeline element. Double-click the card, or click its applied effect name, to edit its shared definition. Strength, size, speed, colors, timing, transition duration, angle, and pattern seed appear where relevant. Definition changes affect every element using that definition; Save to My effects makes a reusable copy.

Graphic passes act on one element at a time. Overlays are restricted to its existing alpha. A full-scene vignette, flare, particles, or grid therefore belongs on a background plate or already composed scene. Red/black silhouettes need transparent isolated drawings; applying them to a flattened image colors the whole rectangle. Iris and panel reveals expose layers underneath. Apply the incoming preset to the upper element and overlap it with the previous shot on another timeline layer.

Motion curves span the clip's original duration; use short clips for quick impacts. Graphic transition durations use seconds and are capped by the clip duration. Splitting and trimming preserve the original effect time, so a split does not restart an entry flash or jitter sequence. A new independently created clip starts a new effect cycle.

## Limits of the reconstruction

Character poses, blinking, mouth changes, scarf motion, walking/running cycles, drawn speed lines, backgrounds, clocks, captions, and changing illustrations remain authored content. Parallax and multiple picture panels require separate elements. The video alone does not tell us whether a bright patch was painted into the artwork or composited later.

Cutout echoes repeat the current drawing at spatial offsets; they do not sample previous video frames. Impact/whip smear uses blur, scale, RGB separation, or spatial echoes, rather than optical-flow motion blur. Angled picture plane is a perspective transform of a flat element. Title breakup uses deterministic triangles rather than the original hand-animated shards. Soft iris is a reusable approximation of the observed soft masked reveal. Exact masks, irregular panel layouts, per-character text choreography, and beat-synchronized editing require composition work beyond a preset.

## Implementation and portability

The catalog is in src/builtin-effects.ts. src/GraphicEffects.tsx implements bounded declarative SVG passes shared by the timeline, sample previews, and Remotion exports. Noise, fragments, and particles derive from clip time and a seed; seeking produces the same frame. Existing projects keep only the definitions actually applied, so the gallery does not populate project JSON or My effects with unused presets.

The effect schema adds optional visual passes, contrast/saturation/letterSpacing/rotateY channels, and ease-out/shake/steps curves. Up to six passes may belong to one definition. Graphic passes run in listed order, then element-level CSS transforms and color/blur channels apply. Existing curve-only effects retain the frameforge-curves-v1 bundle engine. Recipes requiring the extended renderer use kamikami-visual-v2 in the same version-1 bundle envelope, allowing older applications to reject unsupported bundles explicitly. Open project JSON containing the new passes with this version or newer; older project readers can discard fields they do not understand.

Automated tests cover preset validity, library membership, JSON and bundle round trips, saved/imported copies, engine compatibility, zero/disabled behavior, deterministic rendering markup, transition boundaries, and split continuity. Browser pixel review and an actual video export still need verification on a machine where Chromium can launch; the development sandbox blocked browser launch during implementation.
